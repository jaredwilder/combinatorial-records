## Exact structure of a hypothetical 20-cover

Let `r_x` be the number of blocks containing point `x`, and `r_xy` the number containing pair `{x,y}`.

### 1. Point degrees

Every point has

\[
r_x\ge 9.
\]

Since a 20-cover has 120 point incidences in total, the degree multiset can only be

\[
\boxed{(12,9^{12})},
\]

\[
\boxed{(11,10,9^{11})},
\]

or

\[
\boxed{(10,10,10,9^{10})}.
\]

Thus at least ten of the thirteen points have degree exactly nine.

For every degree-9 point, deleting that point from its nine incident 6-blocks gives an optimal nine-block `C(12,5,2)` cover. Any exhaustive 20-cover search should therefore anchor one such neighborhood and quotient by its automorphisms.

### 2. Pair multiplicities

Every pair satisfies

\[
\boxed{r_{xy}\ge3}.
\]

Indeed, after fixing `x,y`, the blocks containing the pair become 4-subsets that must cover the other eleven points.

Globally,

\[
\sum_{\{x,y\}}r_{xy}=20\binom62=300,
\]

hence the total excess above the minimum three is exactly

\[
\boxed{\sum_{\{x,y\}}(r_{xy}-3)=300-3\binom{13}{2}=66}.
\]

For a point `x`,

\[
\sum_{y\ne x}(r_{xy}-3)=5r_x-36.
\]

In particular, when `r_x=9`, these twelve nonnegative integers sum to 9. Therefore at least three are zero:

\[
\boxed{r_x=9\Longrightarrow\#\{y:r_{xy}=3\}\ge3}.
\]

Consequently the three possible global degree patterns force at least 18, 17, or 15 distinct multiplicity-3 pairs respectively.

### 3. Geometry around a multiplicity-3 pair

Suppose `r_xy=3`. Delete `x,y` from the three blocks containing the pair. The result is three 4-subsets of the other eleven points, and their union must cover all eleven.

They contain 12 incidences over 11 points. Therefore exactly one point is repeated and the other ten occur once. Equivalently:

> **Among the three 4-subsets, exactly one pair intersects in exactly one point; the other two pairwise intersections are empty.**

So among the original three 6-blocks through `{x,y}`, exactly two share one additional point `z`; the third shares no point beyond `x,y` with either.

This is a rigid local SAT/ILP branching template, not a heuristic.

A correct next search should:

1. anchor a degree-9 point;
2. classify / enumerate optimal nine-block `C(12,5,2)` neighborhoods up to isomorphism;
3. lift those nine local blocks to six-subsets containing the anchor;
4. select only eleven additional blocks outside the anchor;
5. enforce one of the three exact degree multisets;
6. enforce `r_xy>=3` and total pair excess 66;
7. branch early on multiplicity-3 pairs using the exact three-block intersection template;
8. use triple-level constraints from the audited campaign, not merely point/pair algebra;
9. symmetry-break by the anchored neighborhood automorphism group;

\[
