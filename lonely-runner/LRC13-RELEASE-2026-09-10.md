## Representative exact statements

### Counterexample normalization and covering

A genuinely new counterexample may be normalized to 13 distinct positive speeds

`1 <= u_1 < ... < u_13`, `gcd(u_1,...,u_13)=1`,

with the bad sets `B_u(delta)={t: ||ut||<delta}` covering the circle at `delta=1/14`. Each `B_u(1/14)` has Haar measure `1/7`.

### Deleted-runner trap

For each runner `j`, let

`W_j={t: ||u_i t|| >= 1/13 for every i != j}`.

The known 12-effective-speed theorem gives `W_j != empty`. In a 13-speed counterexample,

`W_j subset B_{u_j}(1/14)`.

Thus any deleted-runner witness that is also `1/14`-safe for the deleted runner closes that tuple.

### Pair-overlap budget

Because 13 bad sets of measure `1/7` cover total measure 1, their pairwise overlap obeys

`sum_{i<j} mu(B_i ∩ B_j) >= 6/7`.

This is structural bookkeeping, not by itself a close.

### Mandatory divisor witnesses

For every integer `q=2,...,14`, a counterexample contains a speed divisible by `q`. Otherwise `t=1/q` makes every runner at least `1/q >= 1/14` from an integer.

### Exact 13-slot law

For a nonmultiple `v` of 13 and phase `tau`, lifted probes are

`t_m=(m+tau)/13`, `m in F_13`.

Writing `v tau=n+beta`, the only possible bad residues satisfy

`r m+n = 0 or -1 (mod 13)`, `r=v mod 13`,

and

`|S_v(tau)| = 2 - 1_{||v tau|| <= 1/14}`.

When two slots occur they are

`{-r^{-1} n, -r^{-1}(n+1)} mod 13`,

with fixed chord displacement `-v^{-1} mod 13`.

### Prime multiplicity law

Let `h_p=#{i:p|u_i}` for any prime `p`. Every counterexample obeys

`p <= (13-h_p) ceil(p/7)`.

Consequences include:

- `h_p >= 12` is impossible for every prime;
- `h_p >= 11` forces `p=2`;
- `h_p >= 9` forces `p in {2,3}`;
- `h_p >= 8` forces `p in {2,3,5}`;
- `h_p >= 7` forces `p in {2,3,5,11,17,23,29}`;
- every prime `p>=31` divides at most six speeds.

In particular the `p=13` multiplicity satisfies `1 <= h_13 <= 6`.

### Majority-GCD hierarchy

For every hypothetical counterexample:

- every 12-speed subset has gcd 1;
- every 11-speed subset has gcd a power of 2;
- every 10- or 9-speed subset has gcd radical dividing 6;
- every 8-speed subset has gcd radical dividing 30;
- every 7-speed subset has gcd radical dividing `2*3*5*11*17*23*29`.

The six-speed level is where this prime-grid argument stops giving a universal prime restriction.
